What Does a Mobile Charger Do?

A mobile charger is a miniature power supply that converts dangerous high-voltage AC from the wall into safe, regulated low-voltage DC — then delivers it to your phone's battery through a precise chemical process.

Switched-Mode Power Supply (SMPS)

Modern chargers use a Switched-Mode Power Supply — far smaller and more efficient than old linear supplies because the transformer operates at 50–150 kHz instead of 50 Hz.

  • EMI Filter: Suppresses high-frequency noise. Uses capacitors + inductors in an LC network.
  • Bridge Rectifier: 4 diodes convert AC to pulsed DC. Full-wave rectification doubles efficiency vs half-wave.
  • PWM Switch (MOSFET): Chops DC at ~100 kHz, creating a high-frequency square wave for the transformer.
  • HF Transformer: At high frequency a tiny ferrite-core transformer steps voltage down with near-zero core loss.
  • Output Stage: Secondary diode + capacitor smooth output to stable DC for the phone.
P = V · I  ·  η ≈ 90–95%

SMPS efficiency (η) vs. linear regulator efficiency of ~30–50%

Rectifier & Smoothing Filter

Voltage Regulation

Raw rectified voltage fluctuates with load changes. A closed-loop feedback system continuously monitors the output and corrects the PWM duty cycle to maintain constant voltage.

  • Voltage Divider: Samples a fraction of Vout for comparison with the reference (Vref = 2.5 V).
  • Error Amplifier: Compares sample to Vref and generates an error signal.
  • Optocoupler: Electrically isolates the feedback from the high-voltage primary side using light (LED → phototransistor).
  • PWM Controller: Adjusts MOSFET on-time based on the error, closing the regulation loop.
Vout = Vref × (1 + R1/R2)

Li-ion Battery Chemistry

Most phones use a Lithium-Ion (Li-ion) cell. During charging, Li⁺ ions are extracted from the cathode (LiCoO₂) and intercalate into the graphite anode layers.

Phase 1 – CC
Constant Current

Charger forces a fixed current (e.g., 2 A) into the cell. Li⁺ ions flow rapidly through electrolyte. Voltage rises from ~3.0 V → 4.2 V.

Phase 2 – CV
Constant Voltage

Charger holds 4.2 V. Ion flux slows as anode fills. Current tapers to <0.05 C, preventing lithium plating and extending cycle life.
