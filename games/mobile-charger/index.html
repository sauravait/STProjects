What Does a Mobile Charger Do?

A mobile charger converts the high-voltage AC from your wall outlet into low-voltage DC electricity that safely charges your phone's battery.

🔌 Input 100–240 V AC
📱 Output 5–20 V DC
⚡ Fast Charge Up to 120 W
🔋 Standard USB-PD / QC

Switched-Mode Power Supply (SMPS)

Modern chargers use a Switched-Mode Power Supply — far smaller and more efficient than old linear supplies.

  • EMI Filter: Blocks high-frequency noise from entering or leaving the charger.
  • Rectifier: Converts incoming AC to pulsed DC using diodes.
  • PWM Switch: A MOSFET rapidly switches the DC at 50–150 kHz, creating high-frequency AC for the transformer.
  • HF Transformer: At high frequency a tiny ferrite core transformer efficiently steps voltage down.

Voltage Regulation

Raw rectified voltage fluctuates. A voltage regulator keeps output constant regardless of input variations or load changes.

  • Feedback loop: Constantly monitors output voltage and adjusts the PWM duty cycle to maintain the target level.
  • Optocoupler: Electrically isolates the feedback path between high-voltage primary and low-voltage secondary sides.
  • USB Power Delivery (PD): A protocol letting the charger and phone negotiate the optimal voltage (5–20 V) and current.

Battery Chemistry

Most phones use a Lithium-Ion (Li-ion) or Lithium-Polymer (LiPo) battery, charged through a two-phase process.

Phase 1
Constant Current (CC)

Charger pushes a fixed current (e.g., 2 A) into the battery until it reaches ~4.2 V. Battery fills quickly.

Phase 2
Constant Voltage (CV)

Charger holds voltage at 4.2 V while current tapers off to ~0.05 C. Prevents overcharge and extends battery life.

3.6–4.2 V nominal cell voltage
