What Is Radio Frequency?

Radio frequency (RF) refers to electromagnetic waves oscillating between 3 kHz and 300 GHz. Unlike visible light, these waves are invisible — yet they carry the music on your radio, the call on your phone, and the data on your Wi‑Fi.

📻 AM Radio 530–1700 kHz
📡 FM Radio 87.5–108 MHz
📶 Wi‑Fi 2.4 GHz 2.4–2.5 GHz

The EM Spectrum

All electromagnetic radiation — from radio waves to gamma rays — travels at the speed of light (c ≈ 3×10⁸ m/s). The key difference is frequency.

Radio waves occupy the lowest-frequency end of the spectrum. Lower frequency means longer wavelength and less energy per photon — which lets them travel vast distances with minimal absorption.

λ = c / f

λ = wavelength (m)  ·  c = speed of light  ·  f = frequency (Hz)

Modulation – Encoding Information

A raw RF carrier wave carries no information on its own. Modulation imprints your voice, music, or data onto it by varying one of its parameters.

Amplitude Modulation (AM)

The amplitude of the carrier changes in proportion to the message signal. Simple to implement but more prone to noise.

Frequency Modulation (FM)

The frequency of the carrier deviates around a centre frequency. Immune to amplitude noise — hence better audio quality.

Phase Modulation (PM)

The phase of the carrier is shifted. Foundational to digital communications (BPSK, QPSK used in Wi‑Fi and 4G/5G).

How Antennas Work

An antenna is a transducer — it converts between guided electrical signals and free-space electromagnetic waves.

  • Transmit: An oscillating current fed to the antenna accelerates electrons, radiating energy outward as EM waves.
  • Receive: An incoming EM wave induces a tiny oscillating voltage in the metal — captured by the receiver circuit.
  • Half-wave dipole: The most fundamental design; optimal length = λ/2, resonating at the target frequency.
L = λ / 2 = c / (2f)

RF Propagation

How RF waves travel from transmitter to receiver depends heavily on frequency and the environment.

🌍
Ground Wave

Low frequencies (below ~3 MHz) follow Earth's curvature. AM radio uses this for long-range coverage.

🌌
Sky Wave (Ionospheric)

Medium/high frequencies (3–30 MHz) bounce off the ionosphere, enabling intercontinental transmission.

📶
Line-of-Sight

VHF/UHF and above travel in straight lines. TV towers, cellular, Wi-Fi, and satellite links rely on LOS.
