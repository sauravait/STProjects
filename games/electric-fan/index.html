What Is an Electric Fan?

An electric fan converts electrical energy into mechanical rotation, driving blades that push air to create a cooling breeze.

⚡ Input Electricity (AC/DC)
🔄 Conversion Electrical → Mechanical
💨 Output Moving Air (Airflow)
🌡️ Effect Evaporative Cooling

The Electric Motor

At the heart of every fan is an electric motor. It uses the interaction between a magnetic field and an electric current to spin a shaft.

  • Stator: The outer fixed coil that creates a magnetic field when current flows through it.
  • Rotor: The inner rotating part (armature) that spins due to electromagnetic forces.
  • Commutator / Brushes: In DC motors, they reverse current direction to keep the rotor spinning continuously.
F = BIL

F = force  ·  B = magnetic field  ·  I = current  ·  L = conductor length

Blades & Airflow

The rotating shaft spins the fan blades. Their angled pitch is the secret to generating airflow.

LowMediumHigh
📐
Blade Pitch Angle

Blades are tilted ~15–20° from vertical. This angle determines how much air is pushed per rotation.

🌀
Number of Blades

More blades increase airflow but add drag. Most fans use 3–5 blades for optimal balance.

Types of Electric Fans

Ceiling Fan

Mounted overhead, these use large blades to circulate air across an entire room. They reverse direction in winter to push warm air down.

Table / Pedestal Fan

Portable and adjustable, these use an oscillating mechanism to sweep back and forth, covering a wide area from a single position.

Exhaust Fan

Mounted in walls or ceilings, they draw stale or humid air out of a room, improving ventilation in kitchens and bathrooms.

Tower Fan

Tall and slim, tower fans use a vertical cylindrical impeller to move air quietly — ideal for bedrooms and offices.

Speed Control

Fan speed is controlled by varying the voltage delivered to the motor. There are two main methods:

🎛️
Resistive Regulator

An older method that uses resistors to drop voltage. Simple but wastes energy as heat.

⚡
Capacitor / Electronic Regulator

Modern fans use capacitors or TRIAC-based electronics to reduce voltage efficiently with minimal energy loss.

🔢
BLDC Motor

Brushless DC fans use an inverter circuit for infinitely variable, whisper-quiet speed control with very high efficiency.
