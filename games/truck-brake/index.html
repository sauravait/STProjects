Overview

Truck Air Brake System

Heavy trucks use compressed air instead of hydraulic fluid to apply brakes. Air brakes are reliable, self-apply on air loss, and powerful enough to stop a fully loaded 80,000 lb vehicle.

Key Components

  • Air Compressor — pressurises air from the engine
  • Air Tanks — store compressed air (100–120 PSI)
  • Brake Chambers — convert air pressure to movement
  • Slack Adjuster & S-Cam — mechanical linkage
  • Brake Shoes & Drum — create friction to stop
Blue = Resting Air Red = Active Pressure
Air brakes use compressed air to push shoes against a rotating drum — stopping the truck safely.

Step 1 of 7

Driver Presses Brake Pedal

Depressing the brake pedal opens the foot valve (treadle valve) inside the cab. This valve controls how much compressed air flows from the reservoir tanks toward the brake chambers at each wheel.

💡

Partial pedal pressure = partial braking. The foot valve is proportional — more pedal travel releases more air pressure for stronger braking.

Pedal: Pressed ↓ Foot Valve: Open
The foot valve opens — allowing pressurised air to flow toward the brake chambers.

Step 2 of 7

Air Pressure Travels Through Brake Lines

Compressed air stored at 100–120 PSI in the reservoir tanks rushes through rigid steel and flexible rubber brake lines to every brake chamber simultaneously.

💡

Air travels at nearly the speed of sound through the lines — brake response is almost instantaneous even on an 18-wheeler with many axles.

Reservoir Pressure
0 PSI
Air flows — notice the colour change from blue (resting) to red (active pressure).

Step 3 of 7

Brake Chamber Pushrod Extends

Pressurised air enters the brake chamber and pushes against a flexible rubber diaphragm. The diaphragm presses on a metal plate, extending the pushrod outward with considerable force.

💡

A typical brake chamber at 90 PSI exerts about 2,250 lbs of force on the pushrod — that mechanical force travels down the linkage to the brakes.

Diaphragm: Pressurised Pushrod: Extending →
Air pressure → diaphragm flex → pushrod extends outward from the chamber.

Step 4 of 7

Slack Adjuster Rotates the S-Cam

The pushrod connects via a clevis pin to the slack adjuster — a short arm on the end of the S-cam shaft. Linear pushrod movement becomes rotational S-cam movement.

💡

The slack adjuster also compensates for brake shoe wear — it can be manually or automatically adjusted to maintain correct pushrod stroke length.

Slack Adjuster: Rotating ↻ S-Cam Shaft: Rotating ↻
Linear pushrod motion is converted to S-cam rotation through the slack adjuster arm.

Step 5 of 7

Brake Shoes Press Against Drum

The rotating S-cam pushes two brake shoes outward. Each shoe is lined with high-friction material. The shoes press against the inner surface of the spinning brake drum, which is bolted to the wheel hub.

💡

The S-cam design ensures both shoes press with equal force simultaneously, providing balanced braking and even wear on the drum surface.

Shoes: Pressed Outward Drum Contact: Active
S-cam rotation forces brake shoes outward — linings contact the spinning drum.

Step 6 of 7

Friction Slows Wheel Rotation

The friction between brake lining and drum converts the wheel's kinetic energy into heat. The drum temperature can exceed 300°C during hard braking. The wheel decelerates and the truck slows.

⚠️

Brake fade warning: Repeated hard braking without cooling can overheat the drums, reducing friction and braking effectiveness. Truck drivers use engine braking on long downhill grades.

Friction: High Heat Wheel: Decelerating
Shoe-drum friction converts kinetic energy to heat — the wheel gradually stops.

Step 7 of 7

Release — Return Springs Reset

When the driver releases the brake pedal, the foot valve closes and the air exhausts to atmosphere. Return springs inside the drum pull the brake shoes back away from the drum, and the pushrod retracts.

💡

Fail-safe design: Spring brakes (parking brakes) work in reverse — strong coil springs apply the brakes automatically if air pressure is lost. Air pressure holds the spring brakes off.

Pedal: Released ↑ Shoes: Retracted Wheel: Free to Rotate
Pedal release → air exhausts → springs pull shoes back → brakes fully released.
