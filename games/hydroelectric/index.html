Hydroelectric Generation

Overview

From Water to Electricity

Hydroelectric plants convert the potential energy of stored water into clean electrical energy through turbines and generators.

  • Reservoir stores gravitational energy
  • Penstock converts height into pressure + velocity
  • Turbine and generator convert motion into power
Water head drives the full generation chain continuously.

Step 1

Reservoir and Intake Gate

The control gate opens to release controlled water flow from the reservoir into the intake tunnel.

Gate opening controls turbine input power.
Gate motion modulates the flow entering the plant.

Step 2

High-Pressure Penstock Flow

Water accelerates down the penstock. The greater the head, the greater the pressure and kinetic energy at turbine entry.

Power scales with flow × head.
Pressure pulses move through the penstock toward the runner.

Step 3

Turbine Runner Rotation

Jet momentum strikes the blades and produces continuous shaft torque.

Hydraulic energy → mechanical energy.
Blade force keeps the shaft spinning under load.

Step 4

Generator Electromagnetic Conversion

The spinning rotor sweeps magnetic flux across stator coils to generate alternating voltage.

Mechanical energy → electrical energy.
A rotating magnetic field creates AC output in the stator windings.

Step 5

Step-Up and Grid Transmission

A transformer increases voltage for efficient long-distance transfer to load centers.

Higher voltage means lower line current losses.
Power pulses travel from plant to transmission towers and city loads.
